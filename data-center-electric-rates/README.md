# Data Center Electric Rates by Utility

How U.S. utilities and regulators are making data centers and other very large customers pay for their own power: every large-load tariff, data center rate class, special contract, rulemaking and law we could find, with docket numbers, status, dates, customer protections, stated household bill impacts and each utility's reported data center load. Every row links to its source.

**Data as of:** October 29, 2026
**Publisher:** [Metro Colo Advisory](https://metrocoloadvisory.com/)
**Dataset page:** https://metrocoloadvisory.com/data-center-electric-rates/
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23839945
**License:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## What's inside

- 105 filings and proceedings across 45 states, plus a multistate PJM proceeding
- Docket number and link, regulator, status, filed date, decision date, and the next scheduled vote, deadline or effective date
- Customer protections in plain words: size thresholds, minimum payments, contract length, collateral and exit fees
- Household bill impacts exactly as stated by the utility, regulator or press, with the source
- Each utility's reported data center or large-load figure in MW, the term it uses (signed, pipeline, queue, forecast) and its system peak, each with a source
- A confidence flag: high = confirmed in a primary document, medium = secondary coverage only

## Limits

- Bill impacts are quoted as stated, not calculated by us, and often cover a whole rate case rather than the data center portion alone.
- Data center load figures use each utility's own definition and are not directly comparable across utilities.
- Status reflects the update date. Check the docket link for the latest.
